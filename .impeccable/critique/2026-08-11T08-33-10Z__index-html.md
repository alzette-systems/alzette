## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Genuinely grounded in this product — an unrelated AI-inference startup could not ship it unchanged. The hero responsibility table, contractual-hosting fact lines, capacity tiers mapped to catalogue rows, and the river mark are woven from this specific offer. The genericness risk is evidentiary, not visual: institutional proof (entity, people, certifications) is absent, and two off-voice spots ("Sneak peek", "the open-weight benchmark leader") leak the leaderboard-marketing register the brand forbids.

**Deterministic scan:** CLI (degraded regex mode) found 2 `side-tab` warnings — `.hosting-line` (index.html:135) and `.closing-line` (index.html:245), both known incumbent styling. Browser overlay run found 39 deduped findings: `low-contrast` ×23 (22 false positives — detector failed to resolve dark ancestor backgrounds; 1 is a 4.50:1 rounding bug), `undersized-ui-text` ×15 (10px functional text on FLAGSHIP tag and modality pills — real but minor), `kicker-above-heading` ×8 (the eyebrow pattern — see tension note), `wide-tracking` ×2, `ai-color-palette` ×3 (cyan-on-dark in dark sections), `overused-font` ×1 (Inter 62% — the design system working as intended, false positive), `side-tab` ×1 rendered.

**Browser overlay:** injection succeeded via the live-server `/source?path=...` route (direct `/index.html` 404s on that server); 77 highlight nodes rendered; server stopped after.

**Tension note:** craft-floor bans kickers above headings and the detector flags 8 eyebrows; the eyebrow is the incumbent world's established device. Recorded as a deliberate-brand decision to confirm, not auto-fixed.

**False positives:** 22 of 23 low-contrast hits, the 4.50:1 boundary flag, and overused-font are detector artifacts. The side-tab hits are mechanically correct; styling is intentionally kept incumbent.

## Overall Impression

The page has found its register: calm, contractual, document-like. The hero finally matches the audience. The remaining gap is not visual — it is verifiability at the exact moments a risk-averse reader checks (footer identity, documentation, status), plus a handful of copy self-contradictions a stress-tester will catch.

## What's Working

1. **The hero responsibility table** — converts "control stays with you" into a document a compliance mind can scan in five seconds; the single most persuasive element, sitting in the first viewport.
2. **Voice discipline** — "defined in the applicable service agreement — not in marketing text", the honest "nothing is sent until you press send" note; the mailto-only form reads as privacy-positive for this audience.
3. **The catalogue table as artifact** — mono context windows, tier columns, modality pills; looks like an internal procurement document, exactly the trust register for fund administrators and PSFs.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P0] No verifiable legal identity at the trust-critical moment.** Footer renders "[TODO: legal entity name…] RCS Luxembourg [TODO: number] [TODO: registered address]"; privacy policy link is `href="[TODO: privacy policy URL]"` (dead). For an audience whose first due-diligence reflex is an RCS lookup, the page says "guaranteed contractually" while offering no entity to contract with. **Fix:** fill entity/RCS/address before any external sharing, or cut the legal strip until known. **Command:** $impeccable harden
2. **[P1] "Documentation" links are mailto, in header and footer.** A nav label promising docs that opens an email composer reads as "the docs don't exist yet" to the developer/IT-partner persona who clicks it. **Fix:** relabel to "Request documentation" (matches the honest "Request the…" CTA pattern) or ship a minimal docs page. **Command:** $impeccable clarify
3. **[P1] Five differently-named doors, one identical room.** "Discuss a pilot", "Request access", "Start a pilot", "Talk to an engineer", "Ask a technical question" all resolve to the same 3-field mailto form; no phone, no booking, no stated response time. **Fix:** consolidate to one verb; add a response-time commitment ("we reply within two business days") next to the form. **Command:** $impeccable clarify
4. **[P2] Copy contradicts the anti-leaderboard stance; units wobble.** Flagship row claims "the open-weight benchmark leader" one screen above "not for the marketing leaderboard"; adjacent rows mix "1M tokens" and "1,040k tokens". **Fix:** replace the leaderboard claim with a workload statement; unify context units. **Command:** $impeccable clarify
5. **[P2] Mobile loses the two most decision-relevant payloads.** ≤640px drops the catalogue's "Best for" column (only workload guidance) and hides the diagram's four control pills, leaving generic boxes. **Fix:** move "best for" into the model cell as a sub-line on small screens; render pill labels as a caption list under the mobile diagram. **Command:** $impeccable adapt

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer):** hero lands instantly — good. No pricing signal anywhere, not even an order of magnitude, so he can't gauge whether this is a €500 or €50,000 conversation before writing an email; "Sneak peek" is the first off-register word he hits; "See how it connects" lands on the dark endpoint section, not the code snippet the label suggests.

**Riley (stress-tester):** catches "benchmark leader" contradicting the anti-leaderboard line; "1M" vs "1,040k" in adjacent rows; "health and status visibility" claimed with no status page linked; Documentation resolving to mailto; retention claim repeated verbatim twice; announce-bar green dot looks like a status indicator with nothing behind it.

**Casey (distracted mobile):** sticky header wraps to ~3 rows and eats a fifth of the viewport; announce-bar "Request access →" hidden below 640px (header button survives); catalogue table loses Tier and Best for — model names and token counts but no guidance; diagram pills hidden. Form itself is fine: 16px inputs, labels present.

**Financial-centre decision-maker (project persona):** the persona the page most needs and least serves at the moment of verification — no RCS number, no legal entity, no registered address, no named human, no phone, no certifications (all visible as [TODO]), privacy policy link dead. He can forward the URL, but the recipient's thirty-second legitimacy check fails at the footer.

## Minor Observations

- Logo links to `#` in header and footer — point to top.
- Missing `og:image`; board-forwarded links render with no visual in chat clients.
- Form has no `action` fallback — with JS disabled, submit silently reloads.
- Duplicate `border`/`border-left` declaration on `.closing-line` (confirmed by detector).
- Header backdrop blur is a mild glassmorphism touch given the anti-reference — subtle, but worth a conscious decision.
- Green dot in announce bar is decorative but reads as "operational" — link a status page or drop the dot.
- Diagram's white card inside the dark section reads as a pasted screenshot, disconnected from the dark card grid below.
- `.resp-head:nth-child()` styling depends on DOM order — fragile.
- "Frontier" in the H1 is the most hype-adjacent word on the page.
- No FR/DE consideration — fine for pilot, worth stating as a conscious choice.
- 10px functional text on FLAGSHIP tag and modality pills (detector, real): bump to 11px.
- 8 eyebrow kickers flagged — incumbent brand device; decision needed, not an auto-fix.

## Questions to Consider

1. If a board member's only verification step is the footer, what exactly are they supposed to check — and what does "guaranteed contractually" mean before a legal entity exists on the page?
2. Do five differently-labeled CTAs that all open the same three-field email composer simulate a larger organisation than the contact surface can support — would one honest door convert better?
3. The page claims "health and status visibility" and "release records are kept" — would linking even a minimal status page or changelog build more trust than any paragraph, or does claiming it without evidence cost more than saying nothing?
